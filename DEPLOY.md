# Deploying to Render (no server to manage)

This assumes you've never used Render before. `render.yaml` in this repo
already describes the whole service — you're just pointing Render at the
repo.

## One-time setup

1. Go to https://render.com and sign up / log in (GitHub login is easiest).
   No credit card required for the free tier.
2. Click **New +** → **Blueprint**.
3. Connect your GitHub account if prompted, and grant Render access to
   `dandiadivyansh-collab/for-claude`.
4. Pick the branch to deploy — either this feature branch
   (`claude/admiring-archimedes-saftfh`) directly, or merge it into `main`
   first and deploy that. Your call; either works.
5. Render reads `render.yaml` and shows you one service
   (`rw-atlas-portal`, free plan). If you want a different, less obvious
   URL slug, change the **Name** field now — it becomes part of the URL
   (`https://<name>.onrender.com`).
6. Click **Apply** / **Create Web Service**. Render runs `npm install`
   then `npm start` automatically. First deploy takes a few minutes;
   wait for the status to say **Live**.

## Get your password

The dashboard is gated by a single shared username/password (HTTP Basic
Auth — your browser will just prompt for it once).

1. Open the service in Render → **Environment** tab.
2. `DASHBOARD_USER` is `zomato`.
3. `DASHBOARD_PASSWORD` was auto-generated by Render — click to reveal/copy
   it. Share both with whoever needs the link.

You can rotate the password anytime: edit `DASHBOARD_PASSWORD` in that same
tab and save — Render restarts the service automatically.

## Get your URL and test it

Your URL is shown at the top of the service page, e.g.
`https://rw-atlas-portal.onrender.com`. Open it, enter the username/password
once, and:

1. Upload today's export.
2. Open the same URL in a different browser (or send it to a teammate) and
   confirm they see the same data after entering the same password — no
   upload action on their part.

## Things to know about the free tier

- **Cold starts.** A free web service spins down after ~15 minutes with no
  traffic, and takes 30-60 seconds to wake back up on the next request.
  Expect that delay on the first open each morning.
- **Disk is not guaranteed persistent** (the risk you already accepted).
  Free services don't get a persistent disk — a redeploy, or certain
  restart/spin-down cycles, can wipe the uploaded data. The server logs a
  warning about this on every startup. If the dashboard unexpectedly shows
  "No data uploaded yet," that's why — just re-upload.
- Pricing and free-tier limits change; double-check current terms at
  render.com/pricing before you rely on this for daily use.

If the free-tier data loss ends up happening often enough to be annoying,
the fix is Render's paid Starter plan (~$7/mo, verify current price) plus
a small persistent Disk add-on — a checkbox in the dashboard, not new
infrastructure to manage.
